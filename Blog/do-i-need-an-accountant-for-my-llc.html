Do I Need an Accountant for My LLC?

Not every LLC needs a full-time accountant. But almost every growing LLC needs a reliable way to keep its books accurate, organized and ready for tax and business decisions.

2026 Guide US Businesses 8–10 min read

The Short Answer

Your LLC may not need an accountant—but it does need accurate records.

If your LLC is simple and you have the time and knowledge to maintain the books correctly, you may be able to handle routine bookkeeping yourself. As transactions, payroll, inventory, multiple accounts, tax complexity or reporting needs grow, professional accounting support becomes increasingly valuable.

Need ≠ Full-Time

You can need professional accounting help without needing a full-time employee.

01 — Start Here

Does an LLC Legally Need an Accountant?

Being an LLC does not, by itself, mean that you must hire an accountant. The right level of accounting support depends on your business circumstances and the records, tax and reporting work you need to manage.

The better question is: “Can I maintain accurate books and meet my business and tax obligations without professional help?”

You may be able to handle bookkeeping yourself if:

  • ✓Your transaction volume is relatively low.
  • ✓You keep business and personal activity separate.
  • ✓You understand your accounting software and basic bookkeeping process.
  • ✓You reconcile your accounts regularly.
  • ✓You have enough time to maintain the records consistently.

02 — The Warning Signs

5 Signs Your LLC Has Outgrown DIY Bookkeeping

01

Your books are consistently behind.

If you keep postponing bookkeeping, your records can become harder to understand and more expensive to clean up later.

02

You cannot trust your reports.

If you are unsure whether your cash, receivables, payables, profit or expenses are correct, the books need attention before they can support decisions.

03

Your transaction volume has increased.

More sales, expenses, payment platforms, bank accounts and credit cards can quickly turn a simple bookkeeping routine into a significant workload.

04

You have payroll, inventory or multiple entities.

These areas can introduce additional accounting processes, reconciliations and reporting requirements.

05

Bookkeeping is taking time away from running the business.

Even if you can do the work yourself, the owner's time has an opportunity cost. Outsourcing can make sense when it frees you to focus on sales, operations and customers.

03 — Match the Professional to the Problem

Does Your LLC Need a Bookkeeper, Accountant or CPA?

You do not necessarily need the most expensive professional for every accounting task. Match the work to the person who is qualified to handle it.

Professional Common Responsibilities Good Fit When...
Bookkeeper Transactions, reconciliations, AP, AR and routine bookkeeping You need accurate day-to-day books
Accountant Month-end close, reporting, accounting oversight and analysis You need stronger financial reporting and accounting support
CPA Tax preparation, tax planning, certain attest and advisory services You need CPA expertise or services requiring a CPA

A common small-business setup:

Routine bookkeeping and accounting support during the year, combined with a CPA for the tax work and specialized services the business actually needs.

05 — Cost

What Determines the Cost of Accounting for an LLC?

There is no universal monthly price for LLC accounting. A simple consulting LLC with limited transactions is very different from an ecommerce company with inventory, payment processors, payroll and multiple bank accounts.

Transaction Volume

More transactions generally require more review and reconciliation work.

Number of Accounts

Multiple banks, credit cards and payment platforms increase reconciliation needs.

Business Complexity

Inventory, multiple entities, multiple locations and unusual transactions can require more expertise.

Book Condition

Clean monthly books cost less to maintain than several years of unreconciled records.

06 — A Flexible Alternative

Does Your LLC Need an Accountant—or Just Accounting Support?

This distinction matters. If your business needs 10–20 hours of accounting work each month, creating a full-time position may not be the most efficient model.

In-House

Dedicated Employee

  • • Useful for consistently high workload
  • • Direct internal availability
  • • Employer payroll and benefits costs
  • • Recruiting and management responsibility

Remote

Flexible Accounting Support

  • • Useful for part-time accounting needs
  • • Can scale for cleanup and close
  • • No need to create a full-time position for every task
  • • Pay based on the agreed scope or hours

The key question:

Do you need a person sitting inside your company full-time—or do you need your accounting work completed accurately and on time?

07 — Decision Checklist

Should You Hire an Accountant for Your LLC?

If you answer “yes” to several of these questions, it may be time to get professional accounting support.

□ Are your books more than one month behind?
□ Do you regularly find unreconciled transactions?
□ Do you have multiple bank or credit-card accounts?
□ Does your LLC have employees or payroll?
□ Do you carry inventory?
□ Do you need monthly financial reports?
□ Are you spending too much owner time on bookkeeping?
□ Is tax season forcing you to reconstruct old transactions?

Important

Bookkeeping and tax work are not the same thing.

An accounting provider can help maintain your books and prepare financial information, while tax preparation, tax planning and other regulated or specialized services may require a CPA or other qualified tax professional. Your business should choose professionals based on the actual service required.

08 — Frequently Asked Questions

LLC Accountant FAQs

Does an LLC need an accountant?

An LLC does not automatically need to hire an accountant simply because it is an LLC. The need depends on the business's transactions, tax situation, reporting requirements, payroll, inventory, number of owners, growth and how comfortable the owner is maintaining accurate records.

Can I do my LLC bookkeeping myself?

Yes. Many small LLC owners handle basic bookkeeping themselves, especially when transaction volume and accounting complexity are low. The important requirement is maintaining complete and accurate business records and meeting applicable tax and reporting obligations.

Should my LLC hire a bookkeeper or CPA?

It depends on the work. A bookkeeper generally handles routine transaction recording and reconciliations, while a CPA may be needed for tax preparation, tax planning or other services requiring CPA expertise. Many LLCs use bookkeeping or accounting support throughout the year and work with a CPA for tax-related services.

Is a remote accountant a good option for an LLC?

A remote accountant can be a practical option when an LLC needs recurring accounting work but does not need a full-time in-house employee. The decision should be based on workload, complexity, communication, controls and the services included.

When should an LLC hire an accountant?

Common triggers include rapid growth, increasing transaction volume, messy or overdue books, payroll, inventory, multiple bank accounts, multiple entities, difficult reconciliations, management reporting needs, or spending too much owner time on bookkeeping.
