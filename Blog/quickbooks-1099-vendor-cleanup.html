Bottom line

If contractor payments are messy now, tax prep will be harder later.

The fastest way to reduce year-end bookkeeping stress is to clean the source records: vendor profiles, bills, expenses, checks, ACH payments, cards, and other transactions. Do not wait until the final tax-prep review to discover duplicate vendors or missing contractor information.

Important: this is a bookkeeping workflow, not a substitute for tax advice. Have your CPA or tax professional confirm filing requirements and final tax treatment.

Problem C

“I paid the contractor” is not enough—trace how the payment entered the books

Year-end cleanup gets difficult when contractor payments are spread across checking accounts, credit cards, bill payments, reimbursements, ACH transactions, or manual journal entries. Review the full payment trail rather than looking at one expense account in isolation.

FAQ

1099 vendor cleanup questions

Why should I clean up my 1099 vendors in QuickBooks before year-end?

Vendor records, payment classifications, missing information, duplicate vendors, and uncategorized transactions can make year-end contractor reporting and CPA review slower and more error-prone.

What QuickBooks vendor information should I review?

Review the vendor name, available business or individual details, tax classification information supplied by the vendor, address and tax ID information where applicable, and the vendor's setup for the intended reporting workflow.

What if a contractor never gave me a W-9?

Do not guess the vendor's tax information. Request the appropriate documentation from the vendor and retain it with your records. Ask your CPA or tax professional about the correct tax treatment and next steps.

Can contractor payments be missed during QuickBooks 1099 review?

Yes. Payments can be hard to identify when vendor profiles are duplicated, transactions are coded inconsistently, or activity is spread across bank, card, A/P, reimbursement, and manual-entry workflows.
