02 — Bookkeeper

What Does a Bookkeeper Do?

A bookkeeper generally focuses on maintaining the accounting records that your business relies on every day and every month.

Bank reconciliation

Match accounting activity to bank statements and investigate differences.

Accounts payable

Organize vendor bills, payments and outstanding balances.

Accounts receivable

Track invoices, receipts and outstanding customer balances.

Transaction coding

Classify business activity consistently in the accounting system.

Best fit

“I need someone to keep my books accurate and current.”

03 — Accountant

What Does an Accountant Do?

An accountant can provide broader accounting oversight beyond routine transaction entry. Depending on the role and qualifications, this can include month-end close, financial reporting, account analysis, adjusting entries, process improvements and management reporting.

01

Month-End Close

Coordinate reconciliations and recurring accounting reviews before reports are finalized.

02

Financial Reporting

Help management understand the balance sheet, profit and loss and other financial information.

03

Account Analysis

Investigate unusual balances, trends and discrepancies.

04

Process Improvement

Build repeatable workflows that reduce errors and improve close speed.

04 — CPA

When Does Your Business Need a CPA?

A CPA is a licensed accounting professional. Depending on the engagement and jurisdiction, CPAs may provide tax preparation, tax planning, attest services and other specialized accounting or advisory services.

Tax preparation

Business and individual tax work as applicable.

Tax planning

Planning around the business's tax position and circumstances.

Specialized services

Certain attest and other services where CPA expertise is appropriate.

05 — Decision Tree

Who Should You Hire?

1. Are your books behind or unreconciled?

Start with bookkeeping or accounting cleanup and reconciliation support.

2. Are the books current, but you need better monthly reporting?

Consider accountant-level support for close, analysis and reporting.

3. Do you need tax preparation or specialized tax advice?

Speak with a qualified tax professional, including a CPA where appropriate.

4. Do you need all three?

You may. Many growing businesses use bookkeeping/accounting support throughout the year and a CPA for tax and specialized services.

08 — FAQs

Bookkeeper vs Accountant vs CPA FAQs

What is the difference between a bookkeeper, accountant and CPA?

A bookkeeper generally maintains day-to-day financial records and reconciliations. An accountant can provide broader accounting work such as month-end close, financial reporting and analysis. A CPA is a licensed accounting professional who may provide tax, attest and other services depending on their license and engagement.

Does a small business need a CPA every month?

Not necessarily. Many small businesses need accurate bookkeeping and accounting throughout the year but use a CPA primarily for tax preparation, tax planning or other specialized services.

When should a small business hire an accountant?

Common triggers include growing transaction volume, multiple accounts, payroll, inventory, multiple entities, overdue reconciliations, unreliable financial reports, a time-consuming month-end close or the need for management-level financial analysis.

Is outsourced bookkeeping cheaper than hiring in-house?

It can be more flexible for a business that does not need a full-time accounting employee. Compare total employment costs, workload, management time, software, benefits and actual scope rather than hourly rates alone.
