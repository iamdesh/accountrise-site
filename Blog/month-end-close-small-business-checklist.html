Quick Answer

A month-end close is the process of finishing, reconciling and reviewing a month's accounting activity so management can rely on the resulting reports. For many small businesses, the close should include reconciliations, A/R and A/P review, required recurring entries, financial statement review and a documented list of unresolved items.

If your business can produce a profit and loss statement but you are not sure whether the cash, receivables, payables and other balances are accurate, the problem may not be the software. It may be the lack of a consistent month-end close process.

A close does not need to be complicated. It needs to be repeatable, documented and reviewed.

01 — Define the Close

What Actually Happens During Month-End Close?

Complete

Record the Activity

Make sure the month's relevant transactions have been entered or imported and obvious exceptions are addressed.

Validate

Reconcile & Review

Compare key balances to supporting records and investigate unusual differences.

Communicate

Report & Explain

Produce reports and highlight significant changes, open items and questions for management.

02 — The Core Checklist

The 10-Step Month-End Close

1

Finish transaction recording

Record and review the month's routine accounting activity.

2

Reconcile bank accounts

Compare accounting records with the relevant statements.

3

Reconcile credit cards

Check statement activity and investigate missing or duplicate entries.

4

Review accounts receivable

Look at open invoices, credits, unapplied payments and aging.

5

Review accounts payable

Check unpaid bills, vendor credits and old balances.

6

Check payroll-related entries

Where applicable, confirm payroll-related accounting is recorded and reviewed.

7

Review inventory and COGS

For product businesses, investigate unusual inventory or cost movements.

8

Review balance-sheet accounts

Investigate loans, fixed assets, equity and other material balances.

9

Review financial statements

Review the P&L and balance sheet for unusual changes.

10

Close and communicate

Document open items and tell management what changed and what needs attention.

03 — The Balance Sheet Test

Don't Stop at the P&L

Income Statement

How did we perform?

Review revenue, major expenses, gross margin where relevant and significant changes from prior periods.

Balance Sheet

Where do we stand?

Review cash, A/R, A/P, inventory, debt, fixed assets and equity so the business understands what sits behind the profit number.

A useful rule:

If a material balance cannot be explained, the close is not finished just because the accounting software shows a completed month.

04 — Common Failure Points

5 Reasons Month-End Close Gets Delayed

01

Late Transactions

Invoices, expenses or other activity arrives late.

02

Unreconciled Banks

Differences are discovered after the close should have started.

03

Old A/R & A/P

Aging balances create questions that were never resolved.

04

Manual Processes

Too much work depends on spreadsheets, emails or memory.

05

No Owner

Nobody is responsible for closing each step.

05 — Better Process

Build a Close Calendar

Instead of waiting until the last day of the month, distribute close tasks across a simple calendar. This reduces the amount of work that has to happen at once.

DAY 1–2

Collect

Statements, source documents and outstanding information.

DAY 2–4

Reconcile

Bank, cards and other key balance-sheet accounts.

DAY 4–5

Review

A/R, A/P, payroll, inventory and unusual balances.

DAY 5+

Report

Finalize reports, communicate exceptions and document the close.

This is an illustrative workflow, not a universal deadline. Close timing should be designed around the business's volume and complexity.

06 — AI & Automation

What Can AI Automate in Month-End Close?

Modern accounting tools can help automate parts of the close—such as transaction imports, recurring entries, matching and workflow reminders.

Automate: repetitive data movement
Assist: matching and exception detection
Review: unusual or material items

Human Check

Automation should make the close faster—not invisible.

Someone still needs to understand exceptions, review significant balances, investigate unexpected changes and communicate the results.

Future-ready principle

Use AI and automation for repeatable work; use people for judgment, controls and business context.

08 — FAQs

Month-End Close FAQs

What is a month-end close?

A month-end close is the recurring process of completing and reviewing accounting activity for a period so the business can produce reliable financial reports. It commonly includes reconciliations, A/R and A/P review, payroll or inventory checks where applicable, financial statement review and resolution of open items.

How long should a month-end close take for a small business?

There is no universal timeline. A simple business may close relatively quickly, while businesses with higher transaction volume, inventory, payroll, multiple locations or complex accounting may need more time. A documented process usually makes the close more predictable.

What should be done before closing the books each month?

Complete bank and credit-card reconciliations, review A/R and A/P, record required recurring entries, review payroll and inventory-related activity where applicable, investigate unusual balances and review the financial statements before finalizing the period.

Why is month-end close important for a small business?

A consistent close helps produce more reliable monthly financial information, identify problems sooner and give management a clearer view of profitability, cash, receivables, payables and other important balances.

Can month-end close be automated?

Parts of the close can be automated, including transaction feeds, recurring entries, matching and workflow reminders. Human review remains important for exceptions, unusual balances and decisions that require business context.
