Why Is My QuickBooks Balance Sheet Wrong?

10 common reasons your balance sheet looks incorrect—and a practical way to find the real problem without randomly changing transactions.

The diagnostic rule
Follow the imbalance.

Start with the account that looks wrong. Then trace the opening balance, recent transactions, reconciliations and linked accounts before changing anything.

A QuickBooks balance sheet can look “wrong” for very different reasons. The fastest fix is usually not to force the report to match a number you expect. It is to identify which account is driving the difference and trace it back to the transaction, opening balance, reconciliation or accounting setup that created it.

The 10 most common causes

01

Unreconciled bank accounts

Book balances can drift from bank records when transactions are missing, duplicated, dated incorrectly or left unreconciled.

02

Incorrect opening balances

A bad starting point can keep an account wrong long after the original transaction is forgotten.

03

Owner draws and contributions

Personal and business cash movements may be posted to the wrong equity or expense accounts.

04

Accounts receivable problems

Old invoices, credits, unapplied payments or duplicate deposits can distort both A/R and cash.

05

Accounts payable problems

Bills and payments that do not properly match can leave liabilities overstated or understated.

06

Loan balances posted incorrectly

Principal, interest and loan proceeds need to land in the appropriate accounts.

07

Inventory or COGS errors

Inventory-heavy businesses can show distorted assets and profit when item, purchase and adjustment workflows are inconsistent.

08

Fixed assets not maintained

Large purchases may be expensed immediately or assets may remain on the books without appropriate depreciation entries.

09

Duplicate or misclassified transactions

Bank-feed duplicates and category errors can move money between assets, liabilities, equity and expenses incorrectly.

10

Prior-period cleanup entries

Historical adjusting entries can affect today’s equity even when the current month appears clean.

How to troubleshoot it in the right order

Use a controlled review instead of editing transactions until the report “looks right.” This sequence reduces the chance of creating a second problem while fixing the first.

01
Pick the problem account.
Cash, A/R, A/P, loans, inventory, fixed assets or equity?
02
Check the opening balance.
Compare it with the prior financials or reconciliation records.
03
Review the activity.
Look for duplicates, unusual dates, large journal entries and unexpected account mappings.
04
Reconcile supporting accounts.
Bank, credit cards, A/R, A/P, payroll liabilities and loans should agree to support.
05
Only then post the correction.
Document what changed, why it changed and which period it affects.

Three balance-sheet clues that deserve attention

CLUE A
Cash does not match the bank

Prioritize reconciliation and transaction duplication before recategorizing expenses.

CLUE B
Equity looks unusually high or low

Review owner activity, opening equity, retained earnings and historical adjustments.

CLUE C
A/R or A/P never clears

Investigate old open items, credits, unapplied receipts and payments rather than writing them off blindly.

What not to do

  • Do not post a random journal entry just to make a total “look right.”
  • Do not delete historical transactions before understanding what they were meant to accomplish.
  • Do not close a period until key balance-sheet accounts have been reviewed.
  • Do not rely only on the P&L. Balance-sheet errors can exist even when profit looks reasonable.
✓

A clean monthly review should leave you with this

Reconciled cash accounts, support for major balance-sheet balances, reviewed unusual transactions, documented adjustments and financial statements you can use for decisions—not just tax filing.

When a cleanup is bigger than a quick fix

If the same differences keep returning month after month, the problem may be structural: weak account setup, inconsistent transaction workflows, old unreconciled periods, or years of corrections layered on top of each other. In that situation, a targeted bookkeeping cleanup and reconciliation project can be safer than trying to repair every account at once.

FAQ

Why is my QuickBooks balance sheet wrong?

Common causes include unreconciled accounts, incorrect opening balances, owner transactions, A/R or A/P issues, loan postings, inventory or fixed-asset errors, duplicate transactions and historical adjusting entries.

Why does my QuickBooks balance sheet not balance?

Accounting systems are designed so assets equal liabilities plus equity. When the report appears inconsistent, investigate account setup and underlying transactions rather than trying to force the totals.

Can I fix the balance sheet myself?

Simple issues such as an obvious duplicate transaction may be manageable. Historical or multi-account problems are better handled with a documented reconciliation and review process.

Should I delete old QuickBooks transactions?

Usually not as a first step. Deleting history can create new inconsistencies. Identify the cause, preserve the audit trail where appropriate, and make a documented correction.
