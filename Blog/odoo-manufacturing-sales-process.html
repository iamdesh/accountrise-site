How to Define a Sales Process in Odoo for Manufacturing Companies

When manufacturing orders are created before the customer order is properly recorded, companies can lose the link between the customer's PO, the sales order, production, shipment and invoice. A stronger workflow starts with the commercial commitment, then connects production and delivery to it.

Executive answer

For most make-to-order workflows, create and confirm the Sales Order before the Manufacturing Order is executed.

The Sales Order represents the commercial commitment with the customer. The Manufacturing Order represents the production work. Keeping the customer PO attached to the Sales Order—and linking resulting production and delivery documents wherever the configured workflow supports it—preserves traceability.

This is a recommended process design, not a universal rule for every manufacturer. Make-to-stock, engineered-to-order, subcontracting and project-based models may require different triggers.

S

Sales Order = commercial source

Captures the customer, PO reference, product, price, quantity, promised dates, payment terms and invoicing expectations.

M

Manufacturing Order = production work

Defines what must be produced, which components are required and how production progress is recorded.

D

Delivery = fulfillment evidence

Connects shipped quantities and delivery status to the correct customer order under the configured logistics process.

I

Invoice = billing record

Should follow the agreed billing terms and configured invoice policy, with clear links back to the commercial order.

Recommended process map

A controlled quote-to-cash flow for a make-to-order manufacturer

Each step has an owner, a document and a control question. Optional stages should be used only when your real process requires them.

1

Inquiry / RFQ received

Sales

Capture the customer's requirements, target quantities, drawings/specifications, requested delivery date and any quality or packaging needs. For repeat customers, confirm approved terms and required customer references.

Control: Do we understand exactly what the customer wants us to quote?
2

Internal estimate / costing review

Optional gate

Estimate material, labor, machine time, outside processing, packaging, freight assumptions and margin. Check bill of materials and routing when available. This can be a formal internal approval step or a lightweight check depending on the company.

Control: Is the quote commercially approved and operationally feasible?
3

Quotation sent and revised

Sales

Maintain versions or a clear revision trail when quantity, price, lead time, specifications or terms change. State the quote reference and validity period, and keep customer communication connected to the quote.

Control: Does the accepted quote match the final commercial agreement?
4

Customer PO verified and attached

Critical control

Check the customer's PO number, legal name, ship-to details, line quantities, unit prices, requested dates, tax/freight terms and any required PO line references. Store the PO file or reference on the commercial record using your approved method.

Control: Can a reviewer connect the customer PO to one definitive Odoo Sales Order?
5

Confirm the Sales Order

Commercial commit

The Sales Order becomes the central operational reference for what was sold. Define a rule for whether each customer PO, PO line, release or approved order grouping requires a separate Sales Order. Avoid creating duplicate or untraceable commercial records.

Control: Does the Sales Order retain the correct customer PO reference and agreed quantities/prices?
6

Trigger replenishment or manufacturing

MRP

For make-to-order products, configure and test the appropriate routes and procurement flow so the confirmed demand can generate or link to the required production work. For make-to-stock, production may be driven by forecasts or replenishment rules instead. Do not assume every product should create an MO automatically.

Control: Can the planner trace each MO or replenishment decision back to demand, without using the MO as a replacement for the customer order?
7

Produce, quality-check and deliver

Operations

Record production and finished quantity, quality checks where used, picking/delivery, lot or serial traceability if required, backorders and partial shipments. Delivery documentation should reference the correct Sales Order.

Control: Do delivered quantities and remaining demand agree with the customer order?
8

Invoice, collect and close the loop

Finance

Create invoices according to the agreed terms and configured Odoo invoice policy—for example, ordered quantities, delivered quantities or an approved milestone arrangement where applicable. Reconcile customer payments and review outstanding A/R.

Control: Can finance trace each invoice to the customer PO, Sales Order and relevant delivery or billing milestone?
Risky pattern

MO first → create Sales Order later

When production is created first and the commercial order is built afterward, teams can end up with missing customer PO references, duplicate Sales Orders, disconnected demand, inaccurate promised dates and unclear links between finished goods, delivery and invoice.

This may be valid in a specific workflow

For example, production can be planned for stock or based on a forecast before a particular customer order exists. The risk is treating an MO as the universal source for a make-to-order commercial commitment.

Stronger pattern

PO verified → Sales Order → linked execution

For customer-specific orders, establish the accepted commercial terms first. Let the configured manufacturing and logistics workflow fulfill that demand while preserving references back to the Sale Order and original PO.

Outcome to aim for

One traceable chain from customer commitment through production, shipment, invoice and payment—plus clear handling for revisions and partial fulfillment.

Question 01

“Should we create the Manufacturing Order first or the Sales Order first?”

For a customer-specific make-to-order workflow, a confirmed Sales Order is usually the clearer commercial starting point. The configured route or procurement process can then generate or link production demand. Make-to-stock production may start from forecasts or replenishment before a customer order exists.

Question 02

“Our manufacturing team creates MOs, but finance cannot find matching customer POs. What should we change?”

Define the PO verification and Sales Order confirmation steps as formal gates. Make the customer PO reference mandatory in the business process, define the rule for one PO versus multiple Sales Orders, and test that production, delivery and invoices retain traceable links.

Question 03

“Can an MO automatically create a Sales Order?”

Do not assume that is the desired standard workflow. Odoo behavior depends on installed apps, customization and integration design. First identify the true business source: customer demand, stock replenishment, rework or internal production. Then configure traceability around that source instead of creating duplicate commercial records.

FAQ

Odoo manufacturing sales workflow questions

What is the recommended sales process in Odoo for a manufacturing company?+

A common customer-specific process is inquiry, internal costing if required, quotation, customer PO verification, Sales Order confirmation, configured procurement/manufacturing, delivery, invoicing and payment reconciliation. The exact sequence depends on the production model and Odoo setup.

Should the Sales Order be created before the Manufacturing Order?+

For make-to-order production, the Sales Order is often the clearest commercial demand source, and configured routes may create or link the Manufacturing Order from that demand. Make-to-stock manufacturing may be planned before customer orders exist.

How should a customer PO be linked to an Odoo Sales Order?+

Define a standard for recording the customer PO number and retaining the source document or required reference on the Sales Order. Decide whether each PO, PO release or approved grouping maps to a separate order, and test that invoice and delivery records remain traceable.

Can all Manufacturing Orders be tied to customer Sales Orders?+

Not necessarily. Make-to-stock production, replenishment, internal use, rework or other manufacturing scenarios may exist without a specific customer order. Your process should distinguish customer-driven production from stock-driven production.

How should invoices be created for partial shipments?+

Set the invoicing policy to reflect the commercial contract and configured Odoo process. Depending on that setup, invoices may follow delivered quantities, ordered quantities, deposits or approved milestones. Test partial deliveries, backorders and remaining quantities before going live.
