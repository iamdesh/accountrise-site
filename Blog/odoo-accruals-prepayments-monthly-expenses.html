The cash may have left your bank in January, or the vendor invoice may not arrive until February. Neither date automatically tells you which month should carry the expense. The key is to match the cost to the period in which it belongs under your accounting policy.

Quick answer

Accruals and prepayments solve opposite timing problems.

An accrual records an expense already incurred when the bill has not yet been recorded or paid. A prepayment keeps the unused portion of a cost on the Balance Sheet and releases it to expense as the benefit is consumed. The exact entry and schedule depend on transaction facts, your policy and Odoo configuration.

A

Expense incurred, invoice missing

Record the estimated amount in the period the service or resource was consumed, with an appropriate accrual or liability, when required by the accounting policy.

Example: December utilities billed in January.
P

Cash paid, benefit remains

Record the future benefit as a prepaid asset where appropriate, then recognize expense across the coverage period using a supportable allocation method.

Example: Annual insurance paid up front.

Troubleshooting board

Seven reasons monthly expenses may look wrong

01

Expense posted on payment date

A cost paid in advance may have been expensed before the related benefit was consumed.

02

Invoice posted in the wrong period

The bill date may not reflect when the related service was received or consumed.

03

Accrual estimate is stale

An old estimate may remain after the actual bill is posted or the underlying facts change.

04

Reversal missing or incorrect

The accrual and the subsequent vendor invoice can double-count or leave a gap if clearing logic is wrong.

05

Prepaid schedule dates are wrong

Incorrect start/end dates can shift the expense into months that do not match the coverage period.

06

Balance sheet schedule not reconciled

The prepaid or accrual ledger balance may not agree with itemized open schedules and supporting documents.

07

Manual journal bypassed the normal process

A manual entry can change expense and balance sheet totals without updating the schedule used for the reconciliation. Document the entry and adjust the supporting schedule when required.

Straight answers to the questions people ask in accounting forums

Question 01

“I paid my annual insurance in January. Should all of it hit January expenses?”

Not necessarily. If the payment buys coverage across future months, the portion relating to future coverage may remain a prepaid asset and be recognized as expense over the coverage period, subject to your accounting policy and materiality threshold.

Question 02

“The December vendor bill arrived in January. Does December still need an expense?”

If the underlying service or cost belongs to December, an accrual may be appropriate under the applicable accounting framework and policy. Use reasonable supporting evidence, then clear or true up the estimate when the actual bill is recorded.

Question 03

“Why did the same expense appear twice after month-end?”

Check whether the prior-period accrual reversed as intended and whether the actual vendor invoice also posted to expense. Compare the opening accrual, reversal, bill and ending liability before making a correction.

Question 04

“How do I prove that prepaid expenses are correct?”

Keep a schedule by contract or invoice with original cost, coverage dates, monthly allocation, cumulative expense and remaining prepaid balance. Tie its total to the general ledger and investigate stale or negative items.

FAQ

Odoo accrual and prepayment questions

What is the difference between an accrual and a prepayment?+

An accrual generally recognizes a cost that has been incurred but not yet recorded through the usual bill process. A prepayment generally represents a cost paid or recorded in advance for benefits that relate to future periods.

Why are expenses too high in one month in Odoo?+

Potential causes include advance payments posted entirely to expense, late invoices, duplicate bills, a missing accrual reversal, incorrect schedule dates or a manual journal that bypassed the standard process. Trace the source document and the expense period.

How do I reconcile prepaid expenses at month-end?+

Maintain an itemized schedule showing original cost, coverage period, cumulative expense and remaining benefit. Tie its total to the prepaid asset ledger and investigate expired, stale, negative or missing items.

Should accruals always reverse next month?+

Not always. Reversal is one method, but the appropriate process depends on the nature of the accrual, how the actual invoice is recorded and the accounting policy. Avoid double-counting and confirm how the actual bill clears the estimate.

Can every prepaid cost be spread evenly across the year?+

No. Straight-line allocation can be reasonable when it reflects the consumption pattern, but the actual contract, service period, accounting policy and materiality should guide the method.
