How Is Bookkeeping Priced in the US?

Bookkeeping services are commonly priced in four ways: hourly rates, monthly packages, fixed project fees, or the total employment cost of an in-house bookkeeper. Each model can be appropriate depending on the business.

Hourly Bookkeeping

You pay for the accounting time used. This can be useful when workload changes from month to month or when you need targeted support.

Monthly Bookkeeping

A recurring monthly fee can provide predictable budgeting for ongoing bookkeeping and reporting.

Fixed Project

Cleanup, catch-up bookkeeping, or a specific accounting project may be priced separately from monthly work.

In-House Employee

The business carries salary and employment-related costs regardless of whether workload is high every week.

Hourly vs Monthly Bookkeeping: Which Is Better?

The right pricing model depends on predictability. A business with a stable monthly workload may prefer a recurring package, while a startup or small business with changing requirements may prefer flexible hourly support.

Why Remote Bookkeeping Can Be More Affordable

Many US small businesses do not need a full-time bookkeeper sitting in an office eight hours a day. They need the books maintained correctly, accounts reconciled, questions answered, and financial reports available when needed.

A remote bookkeeping model can match accounting capacity to actual workload. Instead of paying for unused employee capacity, a business can work with a virtual bookkeeping team on an hourly, monthly, or defined-scope basis.

A smarter question for business owners

Instead of asking only “What is the cheapest bookkeeping rate?”, ask “What is the lowest total cost for accurate, reliable books that I can actually use to run my business?”

What Determines the Cost of a Bookkeeper?

Transaction Volume

More bank, card, sales, and expense transactions generally require more bookkeeping time.

Number of Accounts

Multiple bank, credit-card, loan, or payment accounts increase reconciliation workload.

Business Complexity

Inventory, ecommerce, multiple entities, payroll, and complex revenue models can require additional accounting work.

Bookkeeping Frequency

Weekly, biweekly, and monthly workflows require different levels of ongoing attention.

Cleanup Required

Books that are months or years behind may require a separate catch-up or cleanup project.

Software

QuickBooks, Odoo, and other accounting platforms can have different workflows and reporting requirements.

The Cheapest Bookkeeper May Not Be the Cheapest Option

It is tempting to choose a bookkeeping provider based only on the lowest hourly or monthly price. But accounting errors can create a second cost: time spent fixing them.

If accounts are not reconciled, transactions are incorrectly categorized, or old balances remain unresolved, a business may eventually need a separate cleanup project. Accurate bookkeeping from the beginning can reduce that risk.

Value beats price alone

The best affordable bookkeeping service combines reasonable pricing with accurate work, clear communication, reliable deadlines, and a defined scope.

How Much Does a QuickBooks Bookkeeper Cost?

QuickBooks bookkeeping pricing depends on how the company uses QuickBooks. A simple service business with one bank account may require much less work than an ecommerce business with multiple payment processors, inventory, sales channels, and numerous monthly transactions.

When comparing QuickBooks bookkeeping providers, ask whether bank reconciliation, transaction categorization, month-end close, financial reports, cleanup, and ongoing support are included in the quoted price.
